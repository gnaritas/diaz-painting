Interior painting

Walls you’ll live next to every day

Interior paint gets looked at up close, every morning. That’s why the prep takes longer than the painting.

What an interior job covers

A full interior repaint isn’t just walls — it’s everything the light catches:

  • Walls and ceilings, rolled to an even finish with no lap marks
  • Trim, baseboards and window casings — brushed sharp, never feathered
  • Doors, frames and closets, painted on every side that shows
  • Hallways and stairs, done section by section so the house stays livable
  • Nail pops, dings and gaps patched, sanded smooth, caulked and primed first

Rooms we paint most

Living room Bedrooms Kitchen Hallways & stairs Dining room Home office Kids’ rooms Closets

How the finish gets made

Furniture and floors get covered, hardware comes off, corners get cut by hand before any roller touches the wall. Different rooms want different finishes — tell us how you live in the space and the sheen gets picked to match, flat to semi-gloss. When the last coat is dry, the room gets walked before the tape goes in the truck.

One room or the whole house works the same way: prep slow, paint careful, leave the place cleaner than we found it.
